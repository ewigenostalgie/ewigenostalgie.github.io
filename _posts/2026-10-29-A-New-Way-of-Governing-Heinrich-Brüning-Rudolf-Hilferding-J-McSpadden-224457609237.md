이 글을 통해 여러가지를 얻을 수 있지만, 이를 정리하면 다음과 같다. 첫째, 한 개인으로서의 브뤼닝이라는 인물 자체의 인품이다. 이것은 이상적인 가톨릭적 검소함과, 그리스도교의 보편적 명령으로서의 이웃 사랑을 드러낸다. 한 보수 가톨릭 정치인과 마르크스주의적 사민당 의원의 기묘한 우정은 대단하다. 이 글에서 보여주는 후자의 모습이 특히 중요한 이유는, 그 이웃에 있어서 자신과 이념적 지향이 완전히 다른 사람 역시 당연히 포함되었다는 사실이다. 둘째, 정치인으로서의 브뤼닝에 대해 가해지는 일방적인 독해에 대한 공정한 읽기와, 오히려 그가 바이마르 공화국을 지키려고 했지, 몇몇 사학자들이 제시하는 바와 같이 그가 의도적으로 공화국을 무너뜨리려고 한 것은 아니라는 것이다. 첫번째와 두번째 모두 중요하다. 개인적으로 두번째는 언제나 사학적인 논의가 되는 것이지만, 학계의 악의적이라고 할 정도로 일방적인 견해에 대해 이 논문이 제시하는 면밀한 근거와 반박은 매우 유익하다. 그러나 가장 중요한 것은 나에게 첫번째로 다가온다. 이것은 하나의 가톨릭적 ‘전형적 예시(archetype)’를 보여준다. 경망스럽고 저질스러운 요즘의 정치인이 제공하는 그 자신의 인격적인 표상과 달리, 브뤼닝이 제공하는 개인적인 인품은 존경할만 하다. (그러한 점에서 바이마르 시기 독일과 연방공화국의 독일에서 각각 브뤼닝과 아데나워라는 가톨릭 정치인이 여러 영향력을 행사함과 동시에 그러한 종교적 배경이 제공한 개인적인 성향과 믿음은 매우 중요한 것이다.) 덧붙여 곧 멸망할 바이마르 공화국의 위기를 간파하고, 당내 좌파를 통제하려고 했던 힐퍼딩의 ‘관용정책’ 역시 높이 평가할 만하다.

​

하인리히 브뤼닝의 성격에 대한 전기적 묘사는 보통 내향성(학생 시절부터 시끄러운 사교모임보다는 소수와 대화하는 것을 선호), 금욕성(물질에 대한 욕망 적고 사치스러운 파티도 잘 참석하지 않음), 우울함(기본적으로 비관적), 성실함(프로이센적 의무감과 책임감), 완고함 등등으로 표현된다. 특히 그는 내가 보기에, 자신의 시대가 맞딱뜨린 ‘res dura’를 받아들이고 - 이미 자신이 직접 참전했던 제1차 세계대전에서 이것을 깨달았을 수 있다 - 나름 최선을 다한 인물이라고 생각한다. 그와 바이마르 시절 중앙당의 정치적 복잡성을 단순화하여, 부당하게 일방적으로 평가하는 것은 부당하다. 다행히 그에 대해서는 꽤 공정한 평가가 점차 (이 논문도 포함) 나오고 있기에, 그러한 해석 역시 살펴보아야 한다. ‘Since the 1990s, a cottage industry of Brüning biographies have been published to reconsider the Catholic chancellor’s image and political legacy, and all of these try to rescue Brüning from being a villain in the older magisterial histories of the rise of the Nazis. William Patch’s 1998 Heinrich Brüning and the Dissolution of the Weimar Republic was the first scholarly biography of the chancellor to hit the shelves; it expertly defended Brüning’s image contending, “Brüning did intend to restore parliamentary democracy with relatively minor reforms when the economic crisis passed, and … his fall, not his appointment to office, marked the crucial turning point in the dissolution of the Weimar Republic.”’(590쪽) 놀랍게도 William Patch가 쓴 저 전기가 학교 도서관에 있어서 기회가 된다면 읽어볼 생각이다.

​

본격적으로 이제 ‘close relationship between a devout rightwing Catholic and a Marxist theoretician’에 대해 말해보자면, 이것은 정치적인 측면에서나 개인적인 우정이라는 측면에서나 상당한 울림을 준다. 특히 전자에 있어서 이러한 협력은 오히려 브뤼닝이 어떻게 해서든 공화국을 지키려고 했다는 해석에 상당한 근거를 제공하고, 후자는 그 자체로 올바른 가톨릭적 행위이다. 특히 정치적 측면에서 이러한 시도를 조명하는 것은 몹시 중요하다. ‘The extensive communication between Heinrich Brüning, his new cabinet, and the socialist opposition challenges any belief that the breakdown of the grand coalition in 1930 marked the beginning of something wholly new, authoritarian, and undemocratic. The day before Brüning’s new, more conservative cabinet was sworn in, the future chancellor summoned Rudolf Breitscheid, a leader of the SPD parliamentary party, to a private meeting. [...] Although the grand coalition was now over, the socialists and Brüning’s government continued consulting with each other through the same channels that had been used when the SPD was in the cabinet.’ (591쪽) 저러한 ‘1930년 브뤼닝 내각이 새로운 권위주의적, 반민주주의적 시기의 시작이었다’라는 학술적 독해에 대해서 이 논문 저자의 해석을 지지한다. ‘Various historians have painted Brüning as an antisocialist mastermind, but the SPD leadership trusted him, and he trusted them as legitimate negotiating partners.’ (594쪽) 여기에 대한 반박은 모두 기록해서 사용할 만하다. Despite his doubts, Hilferding stuck to toleration. In this, Hilferding’s actions between 1930 and 1932 challenge the scholarly theory that Brüning’s government was a right-wing hatchet job on democracy. Karl Dietrich Bracher argued that from 1930 on there was a top-down “conscious design” to create “a right-wing regime, independent of parties or parliament.” Bracher saw this new regime as intentionally aiming for the “exclusion of the influence of Social Democrats … without considering bringing them into the politically responsible leadership.” Hilferding’s close relationship to Brüning’s government disproves Bracher’s understanding of the politics of this period. Hilferding’s work to | maintain the socialists’ relationship with Brüning preserved the threads tying the conservative chancellor to the SPD, which kept the socialists involved in governmental decisions at the highest level and kept Brüning’s minority cabinet tied to leading parliamentarians in the Reichstag. (601-2쪽) 특히 저 Karl Bracher라는 학자의 해석은 중앙당과 브뤼닝에 일방적으로 매우 부당하게 해석되었는데, 바로 이 논문이 여러 엄정한 사료적인 근거를 제시하여 그러한 단편적인 - 그리고 어떤 의미에서 매우 악의적인 - 평가에 중요한 반박을 제공하는 것이다. Because of the compromise and connections between right and left, Brüning’s cabinet was not the anti-parliamentary government of conservative fantasies or historiographical fears. The government’s emergency decrees turn out not to have been the embodiment of the desires of a would-be dictatorial cabinet, but the product of behind-the-scenes negotiations among cabinet members, the socialists, and party leaders from the Reichstag. All of their viewpoints were discussed and mulled over in the halls of power before emergency decrees were issued. This replacement political process incorporated politicians representing a majority of the Reichstag’s seats, but it excluded radical parliamentarians whose goal was to topple the system rather than to govern. “Toleration is always the lesser evil if it serves the purpose of warding off fascism, preventing the complete elimination of the parliament, and beating back the threat of a coup.” This was Hilferding’s assessment of a process that showed the resilience of Weimar parliamentary system under stress, rallying for one final opportunity to bounce back and preserve the republic. (611쪽) 여기서도 보이는 바와 같이, 브뤼닝의 비상명령 사용은 언제나 사전적으로 야당 의원들과 논의되었다. 그리고 힐퍼딩이 평가하는 바와 같이, 이것은 양극단 세력이 설치던 바이마르 의회주의를 지키기 위한 거의 최후의 수단이었다.

​

여기에 대해서는 그러한 ‘막후정치’가 오히려 반민주주의적인 것 아니었다는 일각의 반응에 대해서도 역시 정교한 반박이 있다. However, Karl Dietrich Bracher savagely attacked Brüning for this underlying belief in the importance of “personal friendships and antagonisms” as the simplistic “reduction of many explanations to the failings or betrayal of individual people.” Even more damning, Bracher argued that Brüning’s approach to politics rested on a fundamentally conservative “apolitical notion of the political.” [...] Bracher’s attack was echoed by Hans Mommsen, who saw Brüning presiding over a technocratic “‘depoliticization’ of governmental work” that was dangerous in such a politically fraught moment. However, one must read Brüning’s belief in “personal friendships and antagonisms” next to his own experiences having worked successfully with colleagues across the political spectrum. Serving on committees and traveling brought Weimar’s politicians together across party lines. (610쪽) / Brüning’s “new way of governing” excluded radicals like the Nazis, the communists, and the DNVP under Alfred Hugenberg but tied the democratically minded SPD to the minority cabinet through Hilferding. One cannot simply judge interwar German democracy against some ahistorical ideal; Capoccia’s “democratic survivors” of the interwar years— Czechoslovakia, Finland, and Belgium—employed similar techniques of exclusion and illiberal repression to preserve their democracies. (611쪽)

특히 이러한 시도에서 중요한 것은, 저자가 주목하는 사민당 의원이자 마르크스주의 경제학자였던 힐퍼딩의 역할이다. ‘When searching for socialist intermediaries to Brüning’s government, however, historians have generally overlooked Rudolf Hilferding in favor of politicians such as Otto Braun or Hermann Müller. [...] Indeed, Pyta overlooked Hilferding so completely that his 817-page monograph Hindenburg: Herrschaft zwischen Hohenzollern und Hitler does not even include Hilferding in the index! (596쪽) 또한 방법론적으로는 기존의 연구가 주목하지 않았던 개개인의 사적인 만남과 관계에도 주목한다. ‘The historian’s uncertainty about how to interpret squiggly addresses in a tattered pocket calendar or one-line records from the Tageszettel has prompted many scholars to discount this kind of evidence as ephemeral. Instead, they root their arguments about politics during the waning years of the Weimar Republic in sources like cabinet minutes, parliamentary papers, speeches, and newspaper articles that supposedly shed more light on the motives and actions of politicians. However, the sheer number of times that Hilferding and Brüning crop up in each other’s schedules is telling for how close their relationship really was.’ (597쪽) These private meetings between Hilferding and Brüning do appear fleetingly in the scholarship, but no one has set them alongside legislative records from the Reichstag to understand these one-on-one negotiations in detail. Instead, most scholars read these meetings as completely unimportant. (599쪽)

​

이 둘의 사적인 관계는 후술할 것이지만, 우선 공적인 관계에서도 각별했다. Brüning frequently met with Hilferding in the Reichstag with messages for the SPD, and, on occasion, the chancellor even passed on letters to specific SPD parliamentarians via Hilferding. Hilferding was also known to invite his socialist colleagues and Brüning together to his house for private meetings. (597쪽) One of Brüning’s biographers, William Patch, writes that he finds it “surprising” that the “chief theoretician of the SPD” was trusted by the leading German Catholic. (599쪽) Hilferding was not simply a behind-the-scenes negotiator, the host for countless confidential meetings, and an economic adviser; he also worked hard as the primary cheerleader for toleration within the SPD. On the one hand, Hilferding shot down attempts to end the policy of toleration from his party’s left. After the disastrous elections of 1930 that saw the Nazi vote share skyrocket, Hilferding haughtily chastised the SPD’s left wing (600쪽) 특히 600쪽의 진술이 보여주는 것은, 1930년 대공황 이후 총선에서 극단세력인 나치당과 공산당이 엄청난 의석수 증가를 보이자, 힐퍼딩은 사민당 내부 좌파를 ‘chastised’하고 브뤼닝 내각에 대한 ‘toleration’을 추구했다는 점이다. 그는 그러한 관용이 극단적인 세력을 권좌에 올리는 것보다 낫다고 생각했다.

​

개인적으로 더욱 깊은 감동을 받은 것은 브뤼닝과 힐퍼딩의 개인적인 관계, 그리고 브뤼닝이 백방으로 힐퍼딩을 돕기 위해 노력했다는 점이다. 이것은 거의 금전적, 인간적 관계 그 모든 것에서 드러난다. 여기 밑에서부터는 그러한 도움을 모두 인용하여, 이데올로기를 초월한 그 무언가를 보여주려고 하는 것이다. Hilferding and Brüning’s relationship casts light on German politics during this era because their connection did not develop merely out of political expediency during Brüning’s chancellorship. Their long-lasting friendship says something about the world of the Reichstag both men inhabited. In 1959, Brüning reflected nostalgically back on the Weimar-era Reichstag: “There were close personal friendships between parties from the right-wing German nationalists to the Social Democrats. These friendships were often crucial for overcoming crises, and they were frequently more important and more decisive than the cabinet’s decisions.” Brüning’s close relationship to Hilferding shows that this was not mere nostalgia but is also accurate. (602쪽) In following Hilferding and Brüning’s biographies, one is inclined to label them close friends: they traveled together, trusted each other, and, as we will see, Brüning poured his heart, soul, and paycheck into trying to save Hilferding from the Nazis. One should note, however, that Brüning rarely used the word friend to describe anyone in archival materials dating to the 1920s or early 1930s. By 1947, Brüning referred to Hilferding as a friend in an affidavit for a war crimes trial.118 Hilferding’s own views on friendship are lost because he was forced to destroy his papers while fleeing the Nazis.119 However, Hans Schäffer, who considered Hilferding a friend and “valued his judgment,” recalled that Hilferding had a particularly “high opinion of Brüning.” Although Brüning used the word friend in his memoirs, he more often described close relationships as based on “loyalty” rather than friendship. For | Brüning, loyalty was crucial both for politics and for one’s personal life. (602-3쪽)

​

Quite understandably for colleagues who were destined to spend a lot of time with one another, “we discarded our party blinders while we were en route and afterwards all really liked each other.”133 These men and their spouses became close during this trip, which is evident in the photos and postcards that they signed together and sent to family members and friends about the world.134 Well into the 1950s and 1960s, Brüning still fondly wrote to Hans von Raumer about this trip to Brazil: “During my illness I often thought about you, as well as your wife, who I specially admired. The memories of our trip to the economic conference in Rio became especially vivid to me.” (604쪽)

​

In fact, the Center Party once dispatched Brüning to The Hague to make their case in person to Hilferding, who was attending an international conference. Brüning also advocated and supported Hilferding’s policies within the Center Party. Although Brüning did not always agree with the finance ministry’s proposals, he took it upon himself to explain the ministry’s reasoning to his parliamentary party and made a point of meeting with Hilferding to explain the Center Party’s positions and concerns. Brüning was an unofficial intermediary and adviser to Finance Minister Hilferding for most of 1929; this prefigured Hilferding’s role supporting Brüning’s cabinet in the early 1930s. (605쪽)

​

Brüning mused about this moment in his memoirs: “The low point of the parliamentary system was abandoning Hilferding and everything that happened through intrigue at his ministry and within the other parties.” Brüning was obviously proud that he had remained loyal to his longtime trusted colleague. Brüning could not have known that his loyalty would be reciprocated by Hilferding many times over when Brüning was chancellor. (605-6쪽)

​

The personal closeness between the two men meant that Brüning understood something about Hilferding’s financial woes during the 1930s, and the chancellor tried to find Hilferding a well-paid sinecure to help him shore up his income. Although evidence from memoirs should be taken with a grain of salt, according to several later accounts, after the Nazi seizure of power when Brüning got word that the Nazis were planning to arrest the SPD’s leaders, he passed along a warning to Hilferding to encourage him and the rest of the Social Democratic leadership to flee the country as soon as possible. Hilferding then fled Germany via Denmark, just missing the stormtrooper contingent that ransacked his Berlin apartment. Brüning was thus allegedly instrumental in helping Hilferding escape. (606쪽)

​

Brüning was himself forced to flee Germany in May 1934, having gotten word that he would likely be killed, but even exile did not keep Brüning and Hilferding separate. After 1934, Brüning traveled around Europe visiting other German émigrés and former Weimar-era politicians. He and Hilferding kept in touch; when Brüning passed through Zurich, he visited Hilferding, and the socialist introduced Brüning to his émigré SPD friends. (606쪽)

​

The British Foreign Office actually debated starting a campaign in the American press to discredit the former chancellor, and this was only abandoned after one of Brüning’s Harvard colleagues argued that the chancellor would not work against their interests. However, Brüning still understood himself to be a political actor and networker in exile who was taken seriously by foreign governments. (607쪽)

​

Hilferding knew that the outlook was grim, and he wrote letters to friends abroad that only “special intervention” could help them escape France. In October 1940, Hilferding turned directly to Brüning with his financial problems: “Just one more thing that is somewhat embarrassing for me to bring up. My means are exhausted; all previous ties have been cut. Therefore, I would be very grateful to you, if you could place some money at my disposal.” Hilferding’s letter anticipated money coming from rich émigré friends, but Brüning immediately wrote a personal check, and his contributions to Hilferding ultimately totaled $300, more than any of the other three major donors aiding Hilferding and his socialist colleagues. (607쪽)

​

In order to mask his role in the rescue attempt, Brüning asked an American banker in New York to take the public lead in pushing for Hilferding’s release. This banker repeatedly spoke to officials at the US State Department, the French embassy, the French delegation to the International Refugee Committee, and even the chargé d’affaires at the German embassy canvassing support. [...] Brüning also directly reached out to his former political acquaintances in France. He contacted Josée Laval, the daughter of Pierre Laval, a former Third Republic prime minister who was serving in the new Vichy government. (607쪽)

​

Only later would news of Hilferding’s suicide in a French prison reach his wife and friends in the United States. This marked a tragic end to this unsung friendship forged within the committee rooms of the Reichstag. Even then, however, Brüning’s trust, loyalty, and affection for the Hilferdings continued throughout the war and into the postwar period, during which he arranged financial help for Rose Hilferding, found her a job at a hospital in Boston, and then spearheaded the effort in 1952 to convince the government of the new Federal Republic of Germany to grant her Rudolf Hilferding’s ministerial pension. (608쪽)

​

One of Heinrich Brüning’s most recent biographers, Peer Volkmann, recounted this attempted rescue of Hilferding’s party, but his narrative limits itself to Brüning’s personal papers. Volkmann then juxtaposes Brüning’s attempt to save Hilferding with Brüning’s work to help the dissenting National Socialist Otto Strasser escape Europe. This contrast intentionally minimizes Brüning’s support of the socialist group, and Volkmann explicitly argues that there were “political limits to Brüning’s willingness to help,” reminding his readers of his overarching hypothesis that Brüning was a “conservative national expatriate” who did not spring to the rescue of all other needy left-wing former members of the Reichstag.163 However, Volkmann’s account misses any long-term understanding of Brüning’s relationship with Hilferding. It minimizes the fact that Brüning reassembled a network of friends and acquaintances from his period in office to save these socialists. (608쪽) → 이러한 학계의 악의적이라고 할 정도로 편향된 독해에 역시 반박이 됨

​

One cannot shoehorn Brüning’s efforts to save Hilferding into any ideological reading. It reflected the natural evolution of a good personal relationship: one that began between two colleagues in the Reichstag, progressed to their becoming advisers and confidants when the other was in political office, and one that ended, tragically, with Brüning’s unsuccessful attempt to save Hilferding’s life. ​

In reconstructing the relationship between Hilferding and Brüning during the era of toleration and placing it into a larger context— not of competing Marxist or Catholic thought, but that of a long-term friendship between two German politicians—this article has taken seriously Brüning’s insight that personal relationships can shape politics. (609쪽)

이러한 모든 시도를 보면, 나는 이념적으로 거의 대극에 있던 보수적 가톨릭과 사민당 맑스주의자가 그 ‘res dura’를 겪으며 보여준 정치적인 타협과 - 이것이야말로, 바이마르 의회주의를 양극단 세력으로부터 지키기 위한 애처로운 시도 중 하나였다 - , 그리고 이것이 정치가 우리에게 구원을 주지 않는다는 점에서 더욱 더 중요한데, 자신의 계명을 엄숙하게 지키는 한 신자의 모습을 보여준다. 그는 비록 저 맑스주의자와 이념적 성향은 다를지언정, 그가 생을 마감하기 직전까지 온갖 금전적 도움과 그를 프랑스에서 구출하기 위해 거의 자신이 가용할 수 있는 모든 인맥을 총동원해 그를 데리고 나오려고 했다. 심지어 그의 생애가 비극적으로 끝난 이후에도 그의 유족을 잊지 않고 계속 도와주었다. ‘둘째도 이와 같다’ - 이 말을 잊지 말라.
