*‘그 작은 책이 “외로운 길을 걷거나 혹은 모두가 다니는 큰길을 외로이 걷다가 …… 마침내 제가 저의 독자라고 부르는 저 단독자, 그 책이 찾고 있는 저 단독자, 그 책이 마치 두 팔을 벌리고 있는 그 사람을 만나는 것”을 말입니다.’ (『성찬대 아래에서』, 쇠렌 키르케고르. 양성진·이창우 譯, 카리스아카데미 刊. 17쪽)*

​

[인용]

​

That is to say, everyone can be that one, with God's help - but only one receives the prize; again, that is to say, everyone should cautiously have dealings with "the others," and essentially only talk with God and with himself - for only one receives the prize; again, that is to say, the human being is in kinship with, or to be a human is to be in kinship with the divinity.

​

But the eternal, which vaults high over the temporal, quiet as the night sky, and God in heaven, who from this exalted state of bliss, without becoming the least bit dizzy, looks out over these innumerable millions and knows each single individual; he, the great examiner, he says: only one receives the prize; that is to say, everyone can receive it, and everyone ought to become this by oneself, but only one receives the prize.

​

The crowd is untruth. Therefore was Christ crucified, because he, even though he addressed himself to all, would not have to do with the crowd, because he would not in any way let a crowd help him, because he in this respect absolutely pushed away, would not found a party, or allow balloting, but would be what he was, the truth, which relates itself to the single individual.

​

The witness for the truth - who naturally will have nothing to do with politics, and to the utmost of his ability is careful not to be confused with a politician - the godfearing work of the witness to the truth is to have dealings with all, if possible, but always individually, to talk with each privately, on the streets and lanes - to split up the crowd, or to talk to it, not to form a crowd, but so that one or another individual might go home from the assembly and become a single individual.

​

And at the risk of a possibly exaggerated caution, I add just this: by "truth" I always understand "eternal truth." But politics and the like has nothing to do with "eternal truth." A politics, which in the real sense of "eternal truth" made a serious effort to bring "eternal truth" into real life, would in the same second show itself to be in the highest degree the most "impolitic" thing imaginable.

​

The truth can neither be communicated nor be received without being as it were before the eyes of God, nor without God's help, nor without God being involved as the middle term, since he is the truth. It can therefore only be communicated by and received by "the single individual," which, for that matter, every single human being who lives could be: this is the determination of the truth in contrast to the abstract, the fantastical, impersonal, "the crowd" - "the public," which excludes God as the middle term (for the personal God cannot be the middle term in an impersonal relation), and also thereby the truth, for God is the truth and its middle term.

​

But never have I read in the Holy Scriptures this command: You shall love the crowd; even less: You shall, ethicoreligiously, recognize in the crowd the court of last resort in relation to "the truth." It is clear that to love the neighbor is self-denial, that to love the crowd or to act as if one loved it, to make it the court of last resort for "the truth," that is the way to truly gain power, the way to all sorts of temporal and worldly advantage - yet it is untruth; for the crowd is untruth.

​

A crowd is indeed made up of single individuals; it must therefore be in everyone's power to become what he is, a single individual; no one is prevented from being a single individual, no one, unless he prevents himself by becoming many.

​

But it is the crowd which has power, influence, reputation, and domination - this is the distinction of life from life, which tyrannically overlooks the single individual as the weak and powerless one, in a temporal-worldly way overlooks the eternal truth: the single individual.

​

\* \* \*

​

‘저 단독자’가 엘리트주의적, 소수에게만 열려있는 비의적(秘儀的)인 이유가 아님이 여기서 드러난다. ‘no one is prevented from being a single individual, no one, unless he prevents himself by becoming many.’ 이와 반대로, 지금 내가 정리하고 있는 몇몇 엘리트주의, 자아 숭배적 수학자와 물리학자 몇 명은 ‘오직 순수한 수학이나 순수한 물리학의 법칙만을 아는 이만이 자연의 비밀과 아름다움을 알 수 있다’라던가 ‘내가 하는 학문만이 고귀하고 우월하며, 이 서클에 속하지 않은 인간은 지적으로 열등하다’ 운운했다. (이 수학자와 물리학자는 역사에서 매우 잘 알려진 인물이다. 그리고 둘 모두 무신론자였다. 이 조건을 달면, 자신의 유한한 학문에 대한 신적인 숭배를 더욱 잘 이해할 수 있게 해준다.) 그들은, 마치 자신이 ‘the single individual’이라고 생각했겠지만, 실상은 키르케고르가 지적한 ‘the crowd’에 불과했다. 이 글에서 키르케고르가 ‘the crowd’가 될 수 있는 것은 고위 관료나 부를 가진 이 역시 막론한다고 한 점을 살펴보면, 그의 근대에 대한 혜안은 놀랍다.

​

반면에 누구나 자신의 유한성을 깨닫는 동시에 모자를 벗어 경의를 표한다면 - 그 뒤에 따르는 약간의 고통과 책임은 분명 있지만 - 그 어떠한 조건도, 어떠한 부나 학력, 위엄도 필요 없이 누구나 ‘the single individual’이 될 수 있다고 말한 키르케고르의 선언은, 사실 그리스도교적인 것의 정점이다. 왜냐하면 그 종교에서 가르치는 ‘유일하게 값없이 주어지는 것’은 은총뿐이기 때문이다. 이것은 매우 보편적인 그리스도교의 선언이지만, 동시에 그리스도교인 자체도 잘 모르고 ‘the crowd’로 퇴락한다.

​

키르케고르는 이 짧은 글을 시작하면서 ‘저 단독자’에게 헌정한다. ‘My dear, accept this dedication; it is given over, as it were, blindfolded, but therefore undisturbed by any consideration, in sincerity. Who you are, I know not; where you are, I know not; what your name is, I know not. Yet you are my hope, my joy, my pride, and my unknown honor.’ 누구나 모자를 벗으면 저 단독자가 즉시 될 수 있다. 그래서 키르케고르는 이 글을 누구인지도, 이름조차 모르는 이에게 보내면서도 ‘그럼에도 당신은 나의 희망이며, 기쁨이며, 자랑이며, 나의 알려지지 않은 영광이다’라고 말할 수 있었다.

​

정확히 180년 뒤 저 위대한 단독자에 대한 한 단독자가 되고 싶어하는 이의 응답: ‘Who you are, I know; where you were, I know; what your name is, I know. And you are my hope, my joy, my pride, and my known honor.’
